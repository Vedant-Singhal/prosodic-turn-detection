Predicting the end of a human turn, from sound alone

A from-scratch model that scores every conversational pause with p_eot — the probability the speaker is done, not just breathing — from audio before the pause only. No ASR, no pretrained weights, CPU only.

The result

English endpointing is 37% faster; Hindi beats silence in-sample

The scorer simulates a live agent: mean response delay at ≤5% interrupted turns, lower is better. The status quo is a blind silence timer — wait a fixed gap after every pause, then assume the user is done; it pays the full 1600 ms on English. Ours reads prosody and acoustic-completion and commits early when the voice has actually finished, at the same interruption budget. Model selection used a Hindi-priority score because the hidden test is mostly Hindi.

The problem & the data

One user turn, several pauses, exactly one true ending

Each WAV is one real user turn from a phone conversation (restaurant bookings, appointments, orders), 16 kHz mono. Every silence ≥100 ms is labelled hold (user continues) or eot (turn is over). We have 100 turns per language, 248 pauses each, with exactly one eot — the final pause — per turn.

Hard causality rule. For a pause at pause_start, features may use only audio in [0, pause_start). The pause's own length is future information (a live agent cannot hear the future) and is never used as a feature. Our feature code enforces this frame-by-frame.

The one insight that shaped every decision

The scorer only records a false cutoff when delay < dur — i.e. the agent speaks before the user resumes. So short holds are free: the user comes back before the agent acts. The model's real job is not to separate eots from all holds, but to rank true endings above the long hold pauses. We therefore optimise the official metric directly — optimising raw AUC actively hurt the score (see run #4 in the runlog).

How it works

43 causal features → Elastic-Net + hard-neg (mid-complexity)

Turn-endings share cross-linguistic prosody: pitch falls or rises while hesitations stay level; loudness fades; the final syllable lengthens; the voice goes creaky. The last speech before each pause becomes 39 prosodic summaries plus 4 acoustic-completion proxies (how surprising the final frames are under a tiny AR(2) fit) — a legal stand-in for “is this phrase finished?” without ASR. Everything is speaker-normalised from the turn so far.

Signal extraction (once per turn)

  • Custom vectorised normalised-cross-correlation F0 tracker (octave-corrected, median-smoothed) — no pretrained pitch model
  • Short-time energy (dB), spectral centroid, zero-crossing rate, spectral tilt
  • Frames used only if they end at/before pause_start — causality by construction

Feature families

  • Pitch / energy / rhythm — final level & slope, fade, lengthening, creak, tilt
  • Causal context — pauses so far, prior-pause durations
  • Acoustic completion — AR residual RMSE & contour curl on terminal energy/F0 (human-requested after Hindi listen)

Model & training choices (human overruled plain L2-LR)

  • Bake-off across mid-complexity models. Elastic-Net, linear SVM + Platt, LDA/QDA, RBF-SVM, small MLPs, and depth-2 HistGB were compared on the official metric with grouped CV. Kernels and nets raised English delay without beating Hindi’s 850 ms floor until hard-neg reweighting.
  • Winner: Elastic-Net logistic (C=0.5, l1_ratio=0.5) with hard-negative weights on holds whose duration >0.8 s (×12) plus milder duration weighting on shorter holds — sparse enough to ignore weak features, expressive enough to demote the dangerous long holds. Selected by Hindi-priority (eng+2·hin)/3.
  • One multilingual model. English+Hindi training improves English and keeps predict.py ready for an unseen folder. dur is a training weight only — never an inference feature.

The honest finding

Why Hindi’s long holds still pin honest OOF at 850

The metric only cares about separating eots from long holds. So we measured exactly that: AUC of true endings versus holds longer than a growing duration threshold. Hard-neg reweighting unlocked an in-sample Hindi win (831 ms); honest OOF Hindi remains at the silence floor.

Human listening notes (longest Hindi holds). On hi__029#2 (1.5 s), hi__051#1 (1.4 s), hi__097#4 (1.0 s) and neighbours, the pre-pause speech shows deep final F0 falls (−3 to −4 st) and large energy drops — they sound finished. True EOTs are a mixed bag: some fall hard, some stay level, some cut off quietly. Ranking by prosody alone therefore puts the dangerous holds at the top of the “fire early” list. Telling “trailing off to think” from “trailing off because done” needs the words; ASR is banned.

Audit of the “always 850” anomaly. After the human flagged that Hindi never left 850 ms, we re-ran official score.py on adversarial prediction files: oracle → 100 ms, never-fire → 1600 ms, silence → 850 ms. The scorer and CSV path are fine; 850 was the silence-grid floor plus a ranking that could not safely take any shorter delay. A counterfactual pinned the bottleneck precisely: zeroing just the 5 hold scores with dur>0.85 s drops the official score to 736 ms — the ceiling is the model’s ranking of those 5 pauses, nothing else. Hard-neg reweighting of holds >0.8 s (×12) then unlocked in-sample Hindi 831 ms (op point th=0.5 / delay=650 ms); honest GroupKFold OOF Hindi remains 850 ms.

Human ↔ coding agent

Who did what

The agent shipped a solid L2-LR baseline; every gain past it came from human steering.

🤖 Coding agent

  • Built the causal feature pipeline & custom NCC pitch tracker
  • Engineered the first 39 prosodic features (rounds 1–5)
  • Grouped-CV harness on the official metric; caught AUC ≠ objective
  • Duration-aware L2-LR baseline and initial deliverables scaffold

🧑 Human (steering + diagnosis)

  • Priority call: hidden tests are mostly Hindi — select models by Hindi-priority, not English vanity
  • Listen pass: audited the ~9 longest Hindi holds; wrote qualitative notes (deep falls that look “done”)
  • Overruled plain L2-LR: demanded a mid-complexity bake-off (Elastic-Net / SVM / RBF / MLP / light GB)
  • Feature push: required acoustic-completion proxies after the wall diagnosis
  • 850 audit: forced adversarial score.py checks (oracle / never-fire / silence) to prove the floor was real, not a pipeline bug
  • Hard-neg push: demanded reweighting of holds >0.8 s — unlocked in-sample Hindi 831 ms (OOF Hindi still 850)
  • Rejected kernel/MLP winners of AUC that worsened the official delay
